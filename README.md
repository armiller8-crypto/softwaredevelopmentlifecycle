# softwaredevelopmentlifecycle
sprint review and retrospective
How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this?
How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?
What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Interpreting user needs involves thoughtful communication, back and forth, between the end user and product owner.(when it comes to agile) I imagine that, many times, the product owner will have to learn a lot about how the end user's business operations and ask thoughtful, provocative questions to get a full answer out of them. They possibly don't understand the software development process. They might not have thought about things like UI or security measures which should or shouldn't be in place upon delivery. For me, if I am a product owner, correctly interpreting user needs and implementing them comes down to thinking of questions that the user might not have thought of and having an ability to let the user see the program as a whole ahead of time. Then translating that information into user stories that are clear and simple. User stories will help break down the program as a whole into digestible parts that the scrum team can work on. 
At this point, I have only developed very simple programs. I have more or less been told the base requirements. In developing programs, I need to think of each thing that the program needs to do. Display welcome screen. Display user login. Display user menu. Get this piece of information from a database if user clicks this. Or get that piece of information if user clicks that. One thing I still don't have a grasp on is when to use which language. But breaking needs down into simpler tasks is akin to creating stories as a product owner. Aside from story creation, I am a sort of scrum master at my current job, so I certainly will use my skills from what I am doing now to develop top tier scrum teams in the future. 
In terms of being a good team member, I think I have to deliver what I say I can deliver. I think I have to be honest about what I can deliver in the first place. I also have to be humble and, almost vulnerable, because I won't ever know everything, and there will always be people who are able to give me feedback so I can learn and grow. I also think I have to give useable feedback to others and celebrate the wins with the team. I also think I have to do my fair share of the difficult work. I owe it to my team to develop myself so that I can be a stronger member of the team in the future.
